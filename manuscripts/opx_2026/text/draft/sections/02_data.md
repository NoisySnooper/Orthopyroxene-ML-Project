# 2. Data

## 2.1 Experimental corpus

The training and held-out test data are drawn from the Experimental Petrology Database (ExPetDB; Hirschmann et al., 2008; curation updated 2025-07-21). ExPetDB aggregates published experimental runs in which silicate-melt + crystal phase equilibria were reversed or approached from both sides under controlled P-T-X conditions, with every experiment tagged by its source publication. We ingest the 2025-07-21 snapshot as the canonical data source for the two opx pipelines (opx-liq and opx-only). Table 1 summarizes the corpus inventory post-preprocessing. SHA256 hashes of the parsed parquet files and of the ExPetDB source XLSX are recorded in `data/hashes.json` dated 2026-04-16 and reproduced in the Data Availability statement (§8) for reviewer verification; the first eight hexadecimal characters of each are `0c15cc4f` (opx_liq.parquet), `c38fa83e` (opx_only.parquet), and `8aebe073` (ExPetDB.xlsx).

We apply two filters during preprocessing, both of which are locked in `config.py` before any model fitting and reproduced here for transparency. First, a pressure ceiling `P_CEILING_KBAR = 100` truncates the upper tail; experiments above 100 kbar are sparse in ExPetDB and lie outside the calibration domain of any classical opx thermobarometer we compare against. Second, we apply the Fe-Mg exchange equilibrium check `KD_RANGE = (0.23, 0.35)` for opx-liq experiments (Roeder and Emslie, 1970; Putirka, 2008), retaining only crystal-liquid pairs with Kd values inside the expected thermodynamic-equilibrium window. The filter thresholds are domain-standard conventions, not our innovations; we report them explicitly because existing ML thermobarometer papers do not uniformly document this step.

The resulting corpora span geologically meaningful ranges (Table 1): opx-liq n = 600 (93 citations) covers 0-40.05 kbar and 850-1660.1 °C at the 1st-99th percentile; opx-only n = 1035 (123 citations) covers 0-60 kbar and 850-1656.6 °C. Opx corpora at this curation date are approximately half the size of comparable clinopyroxene corpora used in prior ML thermobarometer work, reflecting the published literature's historical emphasis on clinopyroxene-hosted experiments. This sample-size constraint is a recurring motif throughout the analysis: opx results are noisier per-regime, more susceptible to single-experiment leverage, and require more defensive statistical handling than would be needed at cpx-scale sample counts.

## 2.2 Preprocessing and feature engineering

Mineral and liquid compositions are normalized to 100% anhydrous oxide totals per standard experimental-petrology convention, after which we derive three feature sets per pipeline and evaluate all three independently. The **raw** feature set uses the normalized oxide fractions directly (SiO2, TiO2, Al2O3, FeO_total, MgO, CaO, Na2O, K2O, with Cr2O3 and MnO added where the pipeline includes them). The **additive log-ratio (alr)** feature set applies the Aitchison (1982) transform with SiO2 as denominator, producing n-1 log-ratios that correctly handle the compositional-data simplex and remove the sum-to-constant constraint that distorts standard regression diagnostics (Aitchison, 1982; Tolosana-Delgado et al., 2019). The **pairwise log-ratio (pwlr)** feature set includes all n(n-1)/2 pairwise log-ratios of the oxide fractions — a redundant but regression-friendly representation that lets tree-based models pick the most informative ratios per split. We do not commit to a single feature set a priori; instead, we evaluate each family + feature set combination under identical resampling protocols and select the winning feature set per (family, track, target) cell based on 20-seed mean test RMSE.

We additionally derive cation-normalized components of the opx structural formula (Al_VI, Al_IV, Ca_Opx_cat_6ox, Cr_Opx_cat_6ox) used in classical thermobarometer equations, matching the Thermobar package's calculation conventions (Wieser et al., 2022, §2.3). These structural components are available to the ML pipeline as additional candidate features but are not forced into the model; feature importance is reported post-hoc via SHAP (§4.5). EPMA noise augmentation is not applied: our sensitivity analysis found that Gaussian composition-noise augmentation degrades test RMSE on all four opx cells and does not rescue Form B bias correction (§4.6). We therefore use `N_AUG = 1` (no augmentation) as the canonical configuration and report the augmentation ablation as a sensitivity analysis.

## 2.3 Citation-grouped train and test split

Publication-level clustering is the dominant source of information leakage in experimental-petrology ML thermobarometers (Jorgenson et al., 2022). Experiments from a single published study share starting materials, capsule geometry, run-product characterization protocols, and systematic biases in pressure or temperature calibration that do not generalize to independent laboratories. Random train/test splitting — the default in most ML tutorials — places many experiments from the same publication into both splits, inflating test-set performance and hiding the true generalization error. Citation-grouped splits (`StratifiedGroupKFold` from scikit-learn 1.8.0) instead hold every publication on one side of every fold while stratifying on the target distribution; we use 10-fold inner CV and an 80/20 outer split, both citation-grouped.

We therefore adopt the citation-grouped cross-validation protocol of Ágreda-López et al. (2024), which uses `StratifiedGroupKFold` with citation as the grouping variable. For each of the two opx pipelines we generate a single train/test split with approximately 80/20 proportions, constrained so that no citation appears in both splits. Within the training split we perform 10-fold cross-validation (also `StratifiedGroupKFold` on citation) to generate out-of-fold (OOF) residuals used for Optuna hyperparameter search, for bias-correction fitting (§3.5), and for the Ridge-stacked ensemble (§3.6). The test split never contributes to any fitting decision and is touched once per canonical seed for the final reported numbers.

The train/test split is seeded 20 times to characterize the sampling noise introduced by the finite citation partition itself (`SPLIT_SEEDS = range(42, 62)`). Canonical seed 42 is the primary reporting seed; the 20-seed distribution is reported for every test-set metric with the mean and standard deviation across seeds, and the per-seed acceptance decision for bias correction is reported as a majority vote in Table 4 and Supplementary Table S9. Test-set pipeline sizes (Table 1) are 174 for opx-liq and 190 for opx-only; train sizes are 426 and 845 respectively. Test-set pipeline sizes and citation counts at each of the 20 seeds are listed in Supplementary Table S1.

## 2.4 Pre-registered pressure-regime partition

Aggregate RMSE on the held-out test set is the primary benchmark metric but can mask strong regime-dependent performance heterogeneity. We therefore pre-registered a four-bin pressure partition with bin edges at 0, 5, 15, 30, and 100 kbar (`docs/preregistration/p_regime_preregistration.md`, locked before any bias-correction fitting):

| Label | P range (kbar) | Petrological context |
|---|---|---|
| `shallow_crustal` | 0-5 | Arc storage, upper-crustal magma chambers |
| `deep_crustal_MASH` | 5-15 | Melting-assimilation-storage-homogenization zone |
| `lithospheric_mantle` | 15-30 | Spinel to garnet peridotite stability |
| `deeper_mantle` | >30 | Asthenospheric mantle and below |

The bin edges are pinned to petrological regime transitions rather than to statistical properties of our test data. The 5 kbar boundary approximates the upper limit of shallow crustal magma storage in most arc systems (Putirka, 2008, Table 3; Blundy and Cashman, 2008). The 15 kbar boundary approximates the mid-to-lower crustal MASH zone where arc magmas are typically modified before ascent (Hildreth and Moorbath, 1988; Annen, Blundy, and Sparks, 2006). The 30 kbar boundary approximates the spinel-to-garnet peridotite transition in the lithospheric mantle (Klemme and O'Neill, 2000; Walter, 1998). We do not claim these are sharp phase-boundary transitions; they are reporting conventions tied to recognizable petrologic regimes, selected before we inspected any regime-stratified RMSE values. The pre-registration document fixes the `P_REGIME_BIN_EDGES_KBAR = [0.0, 5.0, 15.0, 30.0, 100.0]` tuple and the `P_REGIME_LABELS` list in `config.py`; any subsequent modification would be visible in the git history.

Test-set counts per regime are reported in Table 2 alongside each aggregate performance number. For opx-liq the test-set regime n are shallow_crustal 47, deep_crustal_MASH 61, lithospheric_mantle 58, deeper_mantle 8. For opx-only the test-set regime n are shallow_crustal 48, deep_crustal_MASH 76, lithospheric_mantle 37, deeper_mantle 29. Three of the four opx-only regimes and three of the four opx-liq regimes clear the 20-sample honesty bar (§2.5); opx-liq deeper_mantle with n = 8 is the sole regime below threshold and is reported without directional claims throughout the manuscript.

## 2.5 Dual robustness check

Performance comparisons between our ML thermobarometer and classical or external-ML benchmarks at the regime level are governed by a pre-registered dual robustness check. A directional claim of the form "method A beats method B in regime r" ships in the main text only if all three of the following are satisfied:

1. **Axis 1 (test-set sampling noise):** the 95% bootstrap confidence interval on paired (y_true, y_pred) RMSE for method A in regime r does not overlap the 95% bootstrap CI for method B in the same regime. Bootstrap resampling uses 500 replicates with `SEED_BOOTSTRAP = 42`.
2. **Axis 2 (model-fit stochasticity):** the 97.5th percentile of per-seed test-set RMSE for method A across the 20 seeds falls below the lower CI bound of method B. This prevents a single favorable seed on the tail of the per-seed distribution from carrying an apparent directional claim that is not reproducible across seeds. Deterministic method cells (ElasticNet at fixed hyperparameters, Putirka closed-form equations) trivially satisfy this condition because their per-seed spread is zero or one-dimensional.
3. **Honesty bar:** `n_r ≥ P_REGIME_MIN_N_FOR_CLAIMS = 20`.

Failure of axis 1 or axis 2 downgrades the comparison to "competitive with." Failure of the honesty bar downgrades the regime to "sample-size-limited" and suppresses any directional language. This protocol is locked in `docs/preregistration/p_regime_preregistration.md` §4.3 and implemented via `scripts/audits/robust_audit.py`; the pre-registered pass condition for the opx-liq P headline claim is test T15 in the nb03_test_protocol (§T15), which we report as a pass via `results/opx_per_regime_claims_audit_robust.csv`.
